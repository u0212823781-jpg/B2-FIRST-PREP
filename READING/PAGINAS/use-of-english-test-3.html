Part 1 — Multiple-choice Cloze

Exam task: For each gap, choose the correct answer (A, B, C or D).

Learning a musical instrument as an adult

People often assume that learning to play an instrument is something you either do as a child or not at [1], but a growing number of adults are proving that idea [2]. Music schools across the country report a steady rise in adult beginners, many of whom say they simply never got [3] to it when they were younger. Unlike children, adult learners tend to be more self-conscious about making [4], which can actually slow progress if it isn't addressed early on. On the other hand, adults usually bring far more patience and self-discipline to daily practice, qualities that often [5] for a slower physical learning curve. Teachers who specialise in adult students say the biggest obstacle isn't ability but [6]: most people simply give [7] too soon, expecting results within weeks rather than the months or years that mastering an instrument actually [8].

Gap 1

Answer: all “not at all” means “not in any way”.

Gap 2

Answer: wrong “prove wrong” means to show a belief is incorrect.

Gap 3

Answer: round “get round to something” means you finally find time to do something you were delaying.

Gap 4

Answer: mistakes we say “make mistakes”.

Gap 5

Answer: compensate “compensate for something” means to make up for a disadvantage.

Gap 6

Answer: persistence persistence means the ability to keep going despite difficulty; that’s why “giving up too soon” fits right after.

Gap 7

Answer: up “give up” means to stop trying.

Gap 8

Answer: takes we say “it takes months/years” for how long something needs.

Part 2 — Open Cloze

Exam task: Think of ONE word only which fits each gap.

The last bookshop in town

When the last independent bookshop in our town finally closed [1] good last spring, most people assumed it [2] only a matter of time before the building became yet another coffee chain. Instead, a group of regular customers, [3] refused to let the shop disappear completely, got together and raised enough money [4] buy the lease themselves. None of them [5] any real experience running a business, but they were determined to make it work. A year [6], the shop is not only still open but busier than it has ever been, largely because [7] a loyal community that clearly wasn't ready to give up on it. Sometimes, it seems, the best ideas come from people who have no idea [8] they're doing.

Gap 1.
Answer: for “close for good” means to close permanently.
Gap 2.
Answer: was past simple to match the past story (“assumed”).
Gap 3.
Answer: who “who” introduces extra information about people (“customers, who refused…”).
Gap 4.
Answer: to “enough money to do something” is the normal pattern.
Gap 5.
Answer: had past simple for something someone had before now.
Gap 6.
Answer: later / on “a year later” (or “a year on”) means some time after that event.
Gap 7.
Answer: of We say “because of something” to give a reason.
Gap 8.
Answer: what after “no idea” we need a question word: “no idea what they’re doing”.

Part 3 — Word Formation

Exam task: Use the word given in capitals to form a word that fits each gap.

Fast fashion under fire

Concerns about the environmental [1] of the clothing industry have grown rapidly over the past decade, driven largely by a growing [2] of how quickly today's cheap garments end up in landfill. Fashion [3] argue that so-called 'fast fashion' has trained shoppers to see clothes as [4] rather than long-term purchases. Campaigners are calling for greater [5] from major retailers about where and how their products are made, arguing that most customers currently have almost no [6] into the supply chain behind a ten-pound t-shirt. Some brands have responded with [7] materials and repair schemes, though critics remain [8] that these efforts amount to little more than good publicity.

Gap 1. CONSEQUENT
Answer: consequences a plural noun after “environmental”. CONSEQUENT → consequences.
Gap 2. AWARE
Answer: awareness a noun after “a growing”. AWARE → awareness.
Gap 3. ANALYSE
Answer: analysts people who analyse; a plural noun before “argue”. ANALYSE → analysts.
Gap 4. DISPOSE
Answer: disposable a describing word after “see clothes as”. DISPOSE → disposable.
Gap 5. INFORM
Answer: information a noun after “greater”. INFORM → information.
Gap 6. VISIBLE
Answer: visibility a noun after “almost no”. VISIBLE → visibility.
Gap 7. RECYCLE
Answer: recycled a describing word before “materials”. RECYCLE → recycled.
Gap 8. CONVINCE
Answer: unconvinced a negative describing word after “remain”. CONVINCE → unconvinced.

Part 4 — Key Word Transformation

Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence, using the word given. Do not change the word given. You must use between two and five words, including the word given.

Mixed grammar review 3

1. You must wear a helmet on the construction site. COMPULSORY
It a helmet on the construction site.
Answer: is compulsory to wear (It is compulsory to wear a helmet on the construction site.) We use it is compulsory to + verb to say something is a rule that must be followed.
2. I'm sure he didn't forget the meeting. CAN'T
He the meeting.
Answer: can't have forgotten (He can't have forgotten the meeting.) We use can’t + have + past participle to say we are sure something didn’t happen.
3. The company employs over five hundred people. EMPLOYED
Over five hundred people the company.
Answer: are employed by (Over five hundred people are employed by the company.) We use are/were + past participle + by to make a passive sentence and say who does the action.
4. As soon as I saw her, I recognised her. SOONER
her than I recognised her.
Answer: No sooner had I seen (No sooner had I seen her than I recognised her.) We use no sooner + had + past participle + than to say one thing happened immediately after another.
5. Nobody in the class is as tall as Marco. TALLEST
Marco in the class.
Answer: is the tallest student (Marco is the tallest student in the class.) We use the + superlative adjective to say someone is “the most” of a group.
6. It wasn't necessary for us to book in advance, but we did anyway. NEEDN'T
We in advance.
Answer: needn't have booked (We needn't have booked in advance.) We use needn’t have + past participle to say something wasn’t necessary, but we did it anyway.
