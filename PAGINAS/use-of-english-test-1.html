Part 1 — Multiple-choice Cloze

Exam task: For each gap, choose the correct answer (A, B, C or D).

A city break gone wrong

Last month, my sister and I finally made [1] our minds to visit Prague, a trip we'd been planning for over a year. Everything started well: our flight took [2] on time, and the hotel turned out to be even nicer than the photos had [3]. Our problems began on day two, when Klara, our guide, didn't arrive [4] the meeting point. We waited nearly an hour before it became clear [5] to us that the tour had been cancelled, and nobody had thought to let us know. Rather than let it [6] our trip, we decided to explore on our own, and it turned [7] to be the best decision we made all week. By the time we flew home, we had fallen completely [8] love with the city, guide or no guide.

Gap 1

Answer: up “make up your mind” means “to decide”. The word “minds” right after tells us this.

Gap 2

Answer: off “take off” is the normal way to say a plane leaves the ground.

Gap 3

Answer: suggested photos can “suggest” a feeling without saying it directly.

Gap 4

Answer: at “arrive at a place” is the fixed way to say you reach a specific point.

Gap 5

Answer: to “it became clear to someone” is the fixed way to say something became obvious to someone.

Gap 6

Answer: spoil “spoil a trip” is the normal way to say something ruins a nice experience.

Gap 7

Answer: out “turn out to be” means “in the end, it was”.

Gap 8

Answer: in “fall in love with” is the normal phrase.

Part 2 — Open Cloze

Exam task: Think of ONE word only which fits each gap.

A neighbourhood mystery

It wasn't until I'd lived on Elm Street for almost three years that I finally [1] out why the house at number 12 had stood empty the whole time. According to Mrs Patterson, who has lived [2] the street since the 1980s, the previous owner left suddenly one winter and never came back [3] his belongings. Nobody I spoke [4] seemed entirely sure [5] had happened to him, though everyone had their own theory. Some believe he simply moved abroad; others are convinced [6] was something far stranger. Whatever the truth, the house has now been sold, [7] new owners are due to move in next month. I, for one, will be looking [8] the house closely to see what colour they paint the front door.

Gap 1.
Answer: found We say “find out”, which means to discover something.
Gap 2.
Answer: on we say “live on a street” (like “live in a city”, “live at a number”).
Gap 3.
Answer: for “come back for something” means to return to get it.
Gap 4.
Answer: to “speak to someone” is the normal phrase.
Gap 5.
Answer: what we need a question word after “sure”: “what had happened to him”.
Gap 6.
Answer: it we need “it” to talk about what happened: “convinced it was something”.
Gap 7.
Answer: and a simple joining word between the two ideas.
Gap 8.
Answer: at We say “look at something” to mean you direct your attention to it.

Part 3 — Word Formation

Exam task: Use the word given in capitals to form a word that fits each gap.

Volunteering abroad

Every year, thousands of young people choose to spend part of their summer holidays taking part in [1] programmes overseas. For many, it is their first real taste of [2] living, often in a country whose language and customs are completely unfamiliar. Organisers stress that the experience is rarely as [3] as volunteers expect, and that a certain amount of [4] is completely normal during the first few weeks. Despite this, the vast [5] of participants describe the experience as a genuine [6], citing new [7] and a completely different outlook on the world as the biggest rewards. Perhaps [8], many say the hardest part wasn't the volunteering itself, but adjusting back to ordinary life once they returned home.

Gap 1. VOLUNTEER
Answer: voluntary a describing word for “programmes”. VOLUNTEER → voluntary.
Gap 2. INDEPENDENT
Answer: independence a noun after “a taste of”. INDEPENDENT → independence.
Gap 3. COMFORT
Answer: comfortable a describing word after “as…as”. COMFORT → comfortable.
Gap 4. FRUSTRATE
Answer: frustration a noun after “a certain amount of”. FRUSTRATE → frustration.
Gap 5. MAJOR
Answer: majority a noun after “the vast”, making “the vast majority”. MAJOR → majority.
Gap 6. ACHIEVE
Answer: achievement a noun after “a genuine”. ACHIEVE → achievement.
Gap 7. FRIEND
Answer: friendships / friendship a noun after “new”. FRIEND → friendships.
Gap 8. SURPRISE
Answer: surprisingly a word that describes the whole sentence, after “Perhaps”. SURPRISE → surprisingly.

Part 4 — Key Word Transformation

Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence, using the word given. Do not change the word given. You must use between two and five words, including the word given.

Mixed grammar review 1

1. I haven't been to Paris before. FIRST
This is been to Paris.
Answer: the first time I've (This is the first time I've been to Paris.) We use This is the first time I’ve + past participle to say something is happening for the first time. “Been” stays fixed after the gap.
2. Someone stole my bike last night. STOLEN
My bike last night.
Answer: was stolen (My bike was stolen last night.) We use was/were + past participle to say something happened to a person or thing, when we don’t say who did it.
3. 'I'll help you tomorrow,' said Tom. WOULD
Tom said that he me the next day.
Answer: would help (Tom said that he would help me the next day.) In reported speech, “will” changes to “would”, and “tomorrow” changes to “the next day”.
4. It's possible that she missed the train. MAY
She the train.
Answer: may have missed (She may have missed the train.) We use may + have + past participle to guess about something that happened in the past.
5. I last saw him three years ago. SEEN
I for three years.
Answer: haven't seen him (I haven't seen him for three years.) We use haven’t + past participle + for to say how long something hasn’t happened.
6. This exercise is easier than the last one. AS
The last exercise this one.
Answer: wasn't as easy as (The last exercise wasn't as easy as this one.) We use not as + adjective + as to compare two things and say one is less than the other.
