Speaking · Part 3

Collaborative Task

You and your partner will discuss several options and decide which is the best.
Speak together for 3–4 minutes.

1. Useful language

Giving & Developing Opinions

  • From my point of view, ...
  • The way I see it, ...
  • I'd argue that ...
  • It seems to me that ...
  • I tend to think that ...
  • One of the main advantages would be ...
  • What makes this particularly important is ...
  • The main reason I'd choose this is ...

Interacting With Your Partner

  • What's your take on this?
  • How do you feel about that?
  • Would you agree with that?
  • Do you see it the same way?
  • That's a good point, and I'd also add that ...
  • Building on what you've just said, ...
  • I hadn't thought of that, but ...
  • That ties in with what you said about ...

Agreeing & Disagreeing

  • I'd agree with you there.
  • Absolutely, especially because ...
  • That's a fair point.
  • I see where you're coming from, but ...
  • I'm not entirely convinced.
  • That might be true, although ...
  • I'd look at it slightly differently.
  • I can see the advantages, but wouldn't ... be more ...?

Reaching a Decision

  • On balance, I'd go for ...
  • Taking everything into account, ...
  • Overall, ... seems the strongest option.
  • Of the two, I'd be more inclined to choose ...
  • If we had to choose one, I'd probably go for ...
  • Would you be happy to settle on ...?
  • It sounds as though we both favour ...
  • So, shall we go with ...?

2. Build a stronger contribution

Basic

Travelling is useful.

Better

Travelling is useful because you can learn about other cultures.

B2

Travelling can be particularly valuable because experiencing other cultures may help people become more open-minded and independent.

★Opinion → Reason → Development. Don't stop at your opinion.

3. Move the discussion forward

Introducing Another Option

  • Another option worth considering is ...
  • Perhaps we should also look at ...
  • We haven't considered ... yet.
  • What about ...?
  • Shall we move on to ...?
  • That brings us to ...

Connecting Options

  • This is quite similar to ...
  • This ties in with ...
  • Compared with ..., this might be ...
  • The advantage of this over ... is ...
  • These two seem closely related because ...
  • If we compare the two, ...

Prioritising

  • This strikes me as more important than ...
  • I'd place this above ... because ...
  • This could have a greater impact on ...
  • ... seems considerably more useful in the long run.
  • I'd be more inclined to prioritise ...

4. Keep the conversation going

✕Two separate answers

Candidate A: Travelling can be useful because you can experience different cultures.

Candidate B: Learning new skills is useful because it can help you find a job.

Based on discussion topic · Travel
Why might people choose to travel to different places?
Experiencing other cultures
Meeting new people
Learning a language
Trying new food
Relaxing
VS
✓A real discussion

A: The way I see it, travelling could be particularly valuable because experiencing other cultures can make people more open-minded. What's your take on that?

B: That's a good point, although I'd say learning new skills could have a greater long-term benefit, especially when it comes to finding a job.

A: I see where you're coming from. Perhaps we should also consider making new friends, since social connections can be just as valuable.

B: Definitely — and I'd say it also depends on the person's stage in life. For a student, learning a language while travelling might matter more than for someone older.

Listen→ React→ Develop→ Invite

Don't simply wait for your turn to speak.

Sample answer

To my mind, learning new skills would help young people the most, because it makes them more employable in the future. That's a fair point, but I'd say saving money matters just as much, since financial independence is important too. Actually, learning new skills and saving money often go together — you need skills to earn, and money to invest in yourself. What do you think is more important, though? Doing voluntary work could also help, because it builds real experience and confidence. So overall, I'd say a mix of learning new skills and gaining real experience would prepare them best.

6. Reaching a decision

Why might people choose to travel to different places? Now decide which reason is the most important for travelling.
Experiencing other cultures
Meeting new people
Learning a language
Trying new food
Relaxing
Sample answer · about 1 minute

A: On balance, I'd probably say experiencing other cultures is the main reason — it seems to have the biggest impact on how people see the world. Would you be happy to settle on that one?

B: I see your point, but I'm still more inclined toward meeting new people, because of the connections it can create. Trying new food is fun, but I don't think it changes someone as much.

A: That's fair — it might depend on the type of trip. A short holiday could be more about relaxing, while a longer trip abroad might really open your mind through culture and people.

B: True. In that case, could we agree that experiencing other cultures and meeting new people often go together? You can't really have one without the other when you travel.

A: Exactly — so if we had to choose, I think we can agree that the real value of travelling comes from the people and cultures you encounter along the way.

B: Agreed — let's go with that as our final answer.

Suggest· Check· Respond· Negotiate

8. Interaction check

Listen

Respond to what your partner actually says.

Develop

Add reasons, explanations or examples.

Connect

Link your ideas to your partner's ideas.

Involve

Give your partner opportunities to contribute.

Negotiate

Compare ideas before reaching a decision.
