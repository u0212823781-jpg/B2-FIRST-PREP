Writing · Part 1

The Essay

A step-by-step guide to FCE Part 1: how the task works, how to structure your essay, useful language, an annotated model answer, and a practice question with a self-check.

1 · How does it work?

The essay is compulsory (obligatory) — everyone answers this question.

You're given:

  • a task (an essay question)
  • two ideas (content points)
  • you add an idea (content point) of your own
  • all three ideas get their own paragraph

Your job is to develop all three ideas and give your own opinion.

Task type
Compulsory essay
Word count
140–190 words
Register
Formal / neutral
Time
~40 minutes

2 · Essay structure

Some people believe that social media has had a mostly negative effect on society, while others feel it has brought people closer together. Write an essay discussing both these views and giving your own opinion.

Before you write: plan your ideas

What is this task actually asking? Discuss both views (harmful vs. brings people closer) and give your own opinion. Spend 2–3 minutes writing down ideas for each point below before you start writing.

1. Communication — How might social media affect the way people communicate? Positive, negative, or both? Can you think of an example?

2. Mental health — What effect could social media have on how people feel about themselves? What's an example that shows this?

3. Your own idea ✏️ — Think of one more effect of social media, not already covered above. What's your idea, and what example supports it?

4. Conclusion — Look back at your 3 ideas above. Which one do you find most convincing? Your conclusion should weigh up the points and give your opinion, briefly saying why.

Tap an idea to mark it as a favourite — one option in each list is the one the model essay actually uses.

Here's how that task gets turned into each paragraph, step by step — the same task is written out in full in the model essay further down the page.

Paragraph 1

Introduction

Paraphrase the question in your own words and introduce the topic. Don't give your opinion yet.

~30–40 words
The sentence to reword (from the task)

“Some people believe that social media has had a mostly negative effect on society, while others feel it has brought people closer together.”

How to build it
1 · Paraphrase

Don't copy the sentence above — find different words for the same two ideas:

negative effect→damaging to society
brought people closer together→a tool that has genuinely brought people closer.
2 · Show both sides

Show you'll cover both views — save your own opinion for the conclusion. Use an expression like:

“While some believe that…, others feel that…”

✍️ Reworded (what you actually write)

Social media has become an unavoidable part of daily life, and opinions about its overall impact remain sharply divided. While some see it as damaging to society, others view it as a tool that has genuinely brought people closer together.

Paragraph 2

Body — Idea 1

Develop one point (given or your own), with a reason or example to support it.

~50–60 words
How to build it
1 · Pick one point

Choose one point from the task — here, communication.

2 · State the view clearly

Say clearly what the negative view is (damaged real conversation).

3 · Reason / example

Support it with a concrete example (quick messages replacing face-to-face talk).

✍️ Written out

On one hand, critics argue that platforms like Instagram and TikTok have damaged the way we communicate. Face-to-face conversations, they claim, are increasingly replaced by quick messages and likes, leaving many relationships feeling shallow rather than meaningful. Furthermore, constant exposure to carefully curated images can take a serious toll on users' mental health, fuelling anxiety and unhealthy comparison, particularly among teenagers.

Paragraph 3

Body — Idea 2

Develop the other point (or the opposite view), again with a reason or example.

~50–60 words
How to build it
1 · Pick the point

Cover the opposing view or your second point — here, staying connected.

2 · State the view clearly

Say clearly what the positive view is (easier to stay connected).

3 · Reason / example

Support it with a concrete example (video calls with distant family).

✍️ Written out

On the other hand, social media has undeniably made it easier to stay connected with friends and family who live far away. A quick video call or message can maintain relationships that distance would otherwise weaken, something that would have been unthinkable a generation ago.

Paragraph 4

Body — Idea 3 ✏️ your own idea (example)

The third point comes from "your own idea" in the task — you invent it yourself, related to the topic.

~50–60 words
How to build it
1 · Pick your own idea

Choose the third option from the task — your own idea, e.g. career and creative opportunities.

2 · State the view clearly

Say clearly what your idea is (new opportunities social media offers).

3 · Reason / example

Support it with a concrete example (developing skills like photography, turning a hobby into a career).

✍️ Written out

Beyond these two points, social media has also created genuine opportunities that didn't exist a generation ago. Many young people use platforms like YouTube or Instagram to develop real skills, from photography to public speaking, sometimes turning an early hobby into the beginnings of a career.

Paragraph 5

Conclusion

Give your own clear opinion, referring back to the points you made.

~25–35 words
How to build it
1 · Weigh up both sides

Briefly acknowledge the point from the paragraph you're not siding with.

2 · Give your opinion

State clearly which side you agree with overall.

3 · Justify briefly

Add a short condition or reason for your opinion (if used mindfully).

✍️ Written out

In my opinion, while the risks to mental health are real and should not be ignored, the benefits for communication and creativity ultimately outweigh them, provided social media is used mindfully rather than compulsively.

📌 All three body paragraphs are part of the standard structure — the two given points plus your own idea, each developed with a reason or example.

Model essay

Essay question

Some people believe that social media has had a mostly negative effect on society, while others feel it has brought people closer together. Write an essay discussing both these views and giving your own opinion.

Notes

Write about:

  1. Communication
  2. Mental health
  3. ....................... (your own idea)

📌 A B2 First essay covers all three points from the task — the two given (communication, mental health) plus your own idea (marked with ✏️) — each in its own paragraph. That's why the essay has 5 paragraphs in total.

Social media has become an unavoidable part of daily life, and opinions about its overall impact remain sharply divided. While some see it as damaging to society, others view it as a tool that has genuinely brought people closer together.

Body — Idea 1 — Communication (given)

On one hand, critics argue that platforms like Instagram and TikTok have damaged the way we communicate. Face-to-face conversations, they claim, are increasingly replaced by quick messages and likes, leaving many relationships feeling shallow rather than meaningful.

Body — Idea 2 — Mental health (given)

A second concern relates to mental health. Constant exposure to carefully curated images can take a serious toll on self-esteem, fuelling anxiety and unhealthy comparison, particularly among teenagers.

✏️ Body — Idea 3 — Example of "your own idea"

Beyond these two points, social media has also created genuine opportunities that didn't exist a generation ago. Many young people use platforms like YouTube or Instagram to develop real skills, from photography to public speaking, sometimes turning an early hobby into the beginnings of a career.

Conclusion

In my opinion, while the risks to mental health are real and should not be ignored, the benefits for communication and creativity ultimately outweigh them, provided social media is used mindfully rather than compulsively.

Check your understanding

Quick questions on what's just been explained above — no text to search this time, just recall.

1. What should you NOT do in the Introduction?

Answer: B You save your opinion for the conclusion — the introduction only rewords the question and introduces the topic.

2. How many words should the whole essay be?

Answer: B This is given in the ‘How does it work?’ table at the top of the page.

3. In the Body — Idea 1 paragraph, what do you write after stating your view clearly?

Answer: C A view on its own isn't enough — you always add a reason or example to support it.

4. Which phrase can you use in the Introduction to show you'll talk about both sides?

Answer: C This is the exact phrase given in the Introduction card, under ‘Show both sides’.

5. In the Conclusion, what should you add after giving your opinion?

Answer: B The model essay does this with ‘provided social media is used mindfully’ — a short reason, not a new idea.

6. How many paragraphs does a Part 1 essay have?

Answer: C Introduction + Body — Idea 1 + Body — Idea 2 + Body — Idea 3 + Conclusion = 5 paragraphs.

7. How long should you spend planning before writing your essay?

Answer: B A quick 2–3 minute plan is enough to note down ideas for each point, and it makes the actual writing much faster.

8. When brainstorming your own idea (✏️), what should you do with an idea chip you like?

Answer: B Idea chips are there to spark ideas — you still need to write your own version, not copy them exactly.

Check your understanding — Content

1. What does the Content criterion mainly check?

Answer: B Content is about answering the question fully with relevant ideas — grammar and linking words are marked under different criteria.

2. In Cambridge's own words, a high-scoring essay leaves the “target reader”…

Answer: B “Target reader fully informed” is the exact phrase examiners use for a top Content score — the reader gets everything they need from your essay.

3. A sentence that's true and well-written, but doesn't relate to the task at all, is…

Answer: B However well written, an irrelevant sentence doesn't help your Content score — it can even lower it.

5 · Practice

Your essay question

Some people think that young people today spend too much time on their phones, while others believe technology helps them learn and stay connected. Write an essay discussing both these views and giving your own opinion.

Notes

Write about:

  1. Education
  2. Friendships
  3. ....................... (your own idea)

📌 A B2 First essay covers all three points from the task — education, friendships, plus your own idea (marked with ✏️) — each in its own paragraph. That's why the essay has 5 paragraphs in total.

Your task: the 5 paragraphs below are in the wrong order. Number them 1–5 in the correct order:

  1. Introduction
  2. Body — Idea 1
  3. Body — Idea 2
  4. Body — Idea 3 ✏️ (own idea)
  5. Conclusion

Drag the blocks (or use the ▲▼ buttons) to put them in that order, then check your answer.

⠿⠿

In my opinion, while the risk to academic focus is a genuine concern, the benefits for friendships, safety and emotional support are just as important, provided teenagers learn to manage their screen time sensibly rather than letting it control them.

⠿⠿

On one hand, critics point out that excessive phone use can seriously affect academic performance. Constant notifications make it difficult for students to concentrate on homework or revision, and many report feeling distracted even when they intend to study.

⠿⠿

✏️ (own idea) Beyond these two points, phones can also offer real benefits for safety and independence. Many parents feel reassured knowing they can reach their teenager instantly, and apps that share location in real time allow young people more freedom to travel alone while still being just a call away in an emergency.

⠿⠿

Smartphones have become a constant presence in the lives of most teenagers, and opinions about their impact are far from unanimous. While some argue that young people spend far too much time glued to their screens, others believe that the same technology has opened up new opportunities for learning and connection.

⠿⠿

On the other hand, smartphones have made it far easier for teenagers to stay in touch with friends, especially those who live far away or attend different schools. A quick voice message or video call can maintain friendships that might otherwise fade.
