Formal Language

The register examiners expect from an essay: theory tables and practice exercises.

Why the level of formality (register) matters

What examiners check: Have you used the correct level of formality for an essay?

The impact on your grade:

✅Correct formal register → can improve your score
❌Wrong register (too casual) → lowers your score

Key point: Two students can have the exact same ideas — but the one who uses the right register scores higher than the one who writes too informally. It is a serious piece of writing, not a text message to a friend.

1 Contractions → full forms

Essays never use contractions. Always write the two words in full.

InformalFormalExample
don't / doesn'tdo not / does notMany people do not realise the risks involved.
it's / that'sit is / that isIt is often argued that homework is unnecessary.
can't / won'tcannot / will notThis approach will not work for every student.
I'm / they'reI am / they areThey are increasingly aware of the environmental cost.

4 Avoiding "you" — impersonal structures

Essays don't address the reader directly with "you". Use impersonal or passive structures instead.

InformalFormalExample
you / your (general statements)people / one / individualsPeople are increasingly choosing to work remotely.
you can see that…it is clear that… / evidently,It is clear that attitudes have changed considerably.
you should…it would be advisable to… / X should…It would be advisable to introduce clearer guidelines.
active with a vague subjectpassive voiceMore resources should be allocated to mental health support.

Rewrite each sentence without using "you" or "your", then reveal a sample answer.

Sample answerIt is clear that public opinion has shifted in recent years.

2. If you use social media too much, your concentration can suffer.

Sample answerExcessive use of social media can affect concentration.

3. You should always check the source of information before sharing it.

Sample answerPeople should always check the source of information before sharing it.

4. Your attitude towards risk often depends on your age.

Sample answerAttitudes towards risk often depend on age.

Exercise 2 — Active to passive

A quick grammar reminder for this exercise:

Tense in the sentencePassive structureExample
Modal (should / need to) + verbmodal + be + past participleshould introduce → should be introduced
Present simple (needs to / teach)is/are + past participleneed to teach → need to be taught
Past simple (reduced)was/were + past participlereduced → was reduced

Example — Original (active): Volunteers clean the beach every summer. → Rewritten (passive): The beach is cleaned by volunteers every summer.

Now rewrite each sentence in the passive voice, then reveal a sample answer.

1. Governments should introduce stricter regulations on social media companies.

Sample answerStricter regulations on social media companies should be introduced.

2. Schools need to teach students practical financial skills.

Sample answerPractical financial skills need to be taught to students.

3. The company reduced its carbon emissions by 30% last year.

Sample answerCarbon emissions were reduced by 30% last year.

5 Informal intensifiers & fillers to avoid

AvoidUse insteadExample
really / veryextremely / particularly / highlyThis issue is particularly relevant to young people.
so (+ adjective)extremely / remarkablyThe results were remarkably consistent across all groups.
totally / completely (casual tone)entirely / fullyThis argument is not entirely convincing.
honestly, / like, / well,(remove — not used in essays)—
I reckon / I guessI would argue / it seems likely thatI would argue that the benefits outweigh the risks.

Exercise 1 — Identify and fix

Each sentence has one informal filler or intensifier. Rewrite it formally, then reveal a sample answer.

1. Honestly, this is a really important issue for schools to address.

Sample answerThis is a particularly important issue for schools to address.

2. I reckon the new policy will totally change how people commute.

Sample answerIt seems likely that the new policy will entirely change how people commute.

3. The results were so surprising that researchers repeated the study.

Sample answerThe results were remarkably surprising, so researchers repeated the study.

4. Well, most experts agree that the risks are completely overstated.

Sample answerMost experts agree that the risks are entirely overstated.

6 Rephrasing practice

Rewrite each sentence in formal English. Keep the same meaning — these are short and simple, just to check you can spot and fix informal language.

1. It's not fair that kids get so much homework these days.

Sample answerIt is not fair that children are given so much homework these days.

2. A lot of people reckon social media is really bad for teenagers.

Sample answerMany people believe that social media is extremely harmful for teenagers.

3. You can't just get rid of a problem like this overnight.

Sample answerA problem like this cannot be eliminated overnight.

4. Honestly, I think schools should look into this issue properly.

Sample answerIt would be advisable for schools to investigate this issue properly.

5. There's a big chance that things won't change without new laws.

Sample answerThere is a considerable likelihood that things will not change without new laws.

6. You should put up with some discomfort if you want to improve.

Sample answerOne should tolerate some discomfort in order to improve.

7. The study found out that stuff like sleep really affects mood.

Sample answerThe study discovered that factors such as sleep considerably affect mood.

8. I guess governments need to come up with better solutions.

Sample answerIt seems likely that governments need to devise better solutions.
